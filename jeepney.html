A PHILIPPINE ICON

From war surplus to rolling Filipino art.

Few vehicles are as instantly recognizable as the jeepney. Bright chrome, hand-painted signs, names, colors and decorations turned a practical post-war solution into one of the most visible symbols of everyday Philippine life.

Born from surplus military jeeps after World War IILengthened and rebuilt to carry more passengersDecorated with unmistakable Filipino creativity

THE STORY

Filipino ingenuity filled a transport gap.

After World War II, transport was scarce and large numbers of American military jeeps remained in the Philippines. Filipino builders adapted them for public transport: roofs were added, bodies were lengthened and two long benches created space for more passengers.

Over time, the jeepney stopped looking like a military vehicle. Builders and owners added chrome, bright colors, religious images, route signs, names and deeply personal decoration. Manufacturers such as Sarao helped turn the style into something recognized far beyond the country.

What began as practical recycling became a piece of mobile folk art — and for generations, an affordable way for millions of Filipinos to get around.
